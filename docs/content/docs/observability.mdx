The system is observed through <u>Langfuse</u>, self-hosted on a VM of its own.

## Built on Langfuse

[Langfuse](https://langfuse.com) is an open-source observability platform for LLM applications. It
records what a model was asked, what it answered, and everything the application did in between, and
it holds the **prompts** and the **evaluation datasets** alongside those records.

Beyond the traces themselves, it is where the operational numbers live.

- **Traffic.** How many conversations and turns the system is handling, and when.
- **Token usage and cost.** Per turn, per conversation and in aggregate.
- **Latency.** Where a slow turn spent its time, which separates <u>a slow model from a slow
  search</u> and makes a spike traceable to the step that caused it.
- **Errors.** Failed turns and the guardrails that fired, findable without reading logs.

## One conversation, one trace

  src="shots/langfuse-trace.png"

A conversation is a <u>single trace</u>, and each turn hangs off it as spans: the triage verdict,
every tool call with its arguments and its results, the model spans, and the final answer. When
somebody says the assistant gave a bad answer, the question is never "what did the model say" but
"what did it look at".

## Prompts are versioned

<Shot
  src="shots/langfuse-prompts.png"

Prompts are fetched from Langfuse by <u>name and label</u>, with the constant in the source as a
fallback on any error.

Promotion moves the label onto <u>the version that was tested</u>.

## Feedback lands on the turn

The thumbs on a chat message write a score onto <u>that turn</u>, not onto the conversation as a
whole. So a thumbs-down is not a number in a dashboard: it is attached to the exact answer, its tool
calls and the trials it cited, which is what makes it worth reading later.

## What gets captured

Patient-authored text is captured only where the environment allows it. Three places carry it (the
Pydantic AI spans, the turn span, and the observed tools) and all three read the same setting. See [Environments](/docs/architecture#environments).
